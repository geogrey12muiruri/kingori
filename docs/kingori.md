# Dr. King’ori Website
## Project Discovery Document

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 21 October 2026  
**Status:** Discovery draft — pending stakeholder validation  
**Project:** Official digital platform for Dr. King’ori  
**Primary purpose:** Establish the strategic, editorial, functional, and operational requirements that will guide subsequent design and development.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The proposed Dr. King’ori website should serve as the digital home of his conversations, ideas, media work, and wider public-facing activities.

The opportunity is not simply to reproduce an existing YouTube channel or podcast on another website. It is to create a central platform through which audiences can discover conversations, explore subjects, follow ideas across different episodes, engage with written content, learn about Dr. King’ori’s work, and access legitimate professional or commercial opportunities.

The client’s initial direction centres on the idea **“Questions That Matter”**, supported by the statement **“Where curiosity becomes a conversation.”** The intended experience brings together thoughtful conversations, entertainment, humour, contemporary issues, business, money, relationships, politics, and the experiences that shape people and society.

The supplied conversation transcripts reinforce the opportunity. They show discussions that move between practical knowledge, personal experience, public affairs, business, social behaviour, humour, and challenging questions. This range gives the platform an editorial identity that can extend beyond individual video releases.

The website therefore needs to address five connected objectives:

1. **Centralise the brand:** Establish an identifiable official destination for Dr. King’ori’s work.
2. **Improve discovery:** Help visitors find relevant conversations by topic, guest, question, and format.
3. **Extend the life of content:** Make it possible to connect long-form episodes with written articles, selected moments, quotes, and related conversations.
4. **Strengthen audience relationships:** Give visitors clear ways to return, subscribe, suggest questions, and participate where appropriate.
5. **Support professional opportunities:** Provide clear pathways for bookings, guest proposals, sponsorships, advertising, and partnerships.

The website must balance these objectives without allowing commercial features to overwhelm the editorial experience.

This document is a discovery baseline, not a final design specification. Several decisions—including the exact public-facing brand name, the initial launch scope, content ownership, booking processes, and commercial offerings—remain subject to confirmation.

## 4. Brand and Editorial Discovery

### 4.1 The central proposition

The client’s proposed concept is:

**Questions That Matter — Where curiosity becomes a conversation.**

This provides a potential organising principle for the website. It emphasises inquiry and discussion rather than treating the platform as a collection of isolated media files.

The concept should be tested against the full range of Dr. King’ori’s work. It needs to accommodate serious interviews, humour, personal stories, controversial topics, practical business conversations, and social commentary without suggesting that every episode has the same tone or purpose.

### 4.2 The editorial character evidenced by the transcripts

The supplied transcripts demonstrate several recurring characteristics.

**A. Conversations that move beyond the obvious**

The interviews explore issues beneath familiar headlines or everyday assumptions. Discussions can move from money and economic conditions into questions of responsibility, education, social structures, opportunity, or personal choices.

**B. Serious subjects approached through accessible conversation**

The material includes humour, anecdotes, personal experiences, and relatable examples alongside more serious issues. The website should accommodate this combination instead of presenting the platform as exclusively academic, political, or financial.

**C. Interest in how people and systems work**

The transcripts explore both individual behaviour and broader systems: business models, family relationships, political power, economic opportunity, social expectations, and public accountability.

**D. Guest-centred experiences**

Guests bring personal histories, professional experience, opinions, and interpretations. Their backgrounds and the subjects they discuss can become important routes into the archive.

**E. Content with more than one useful entry point**

A single conversation may be relevant to several topics. For example, a discussion about business could also involve education, social mobility, family expectations, or personal discipline.

These characteristics support a topic-led and relationship-led archive rather than a rigid list of unrelated categories.

### 4.3 Editorial principles to validate

The following principles are proposed for stakeholder approval:

1. **Curiosity before categorisation:** Begin with the question or subject that makes a conversation interesting.
2. **Conversation over promotion:** Give the substance of the content priority over promotional messaging.
3. **Context over isolated clips:** Where possible, help visitors find the full conversation behind a selected quote or moment.
4. **Accessibility without oversimplification:** Explain the subject and guest context without removing the complexity of the discussion.
5. **Accurate attribution:** Distinguish the host’s questions, the guest’s opinions, independently verified information, and editorial commentary.
6. **Multiple ways to discover:** Allow visitors to arrive through a topic, person, question, article, or episode.
7. **Editorial and commercial clarity:** Identify sponsorships and paid partnerships clearly and keep their presentation distinct from editorial content.

These are recommendations, not confirmed statements of the client’s editorial policy.

### 4.4 Handling controversial or unverified claims

The supplied transcripts include discussions of politics, economics, religion, business practices, family life, and social behaviour. Some statements are opinions or disputed interpretations; some may have been inaccurately captured by automated transcription.

The website must not automatically present every statement made by a guest as an established fact or as Dr. King’ori’s own position.

Before material is republished as an article, quotation, summary, or social excerpt, the editorial process should establish:

- Who made the statement.
- Whether the statement is a quotation, opinion, allegation, interpretation, or verified fact.
- Whether the original recording supports the wording.
- Whether additional context is necessary.
- Whether the source episode should be linked.
- Whether sensitive claims require editorial or legal review.

The exact review policy requires client confirmation.

## 5. Content and Conversation Analysis

### 5.1 Themes identified in the supplied material

The supplied transcripts support the following broad subject areas.

**Money, business, and wealth**

Conversations examine spending, saving, business models, employment, entrepreneurship, professional work, wealth creation, imports, production, and financial opportunity.

**Power, politics, and accountability**

Discussions explore political leadership, elections, public responsibility, economic history, political narratives, and the relationship between institutions and ordinary citizens.

**People, relationships, and family**

Personal stories include relationships with parents, childhood experiences, forgiveness, family dynamics, and how individual experiences shape later decisions.

**Society, education, and opportunity**

The material raises questions about education, work, social expectations, economic participation, and the structures that influence people's opportunities.

**Belief, morality, and taboo questions**

Some conversations explore religion, public attitudes, controversial ideas, and topics people may be reluctant to discuss openly.

**Life stories, humour, and unexpected experiences**

Anecdotes about work, personal experiences, unusual situations, and humorous moments provide a different entry point into the platform.

These categories describe themes in the supplied material. They do not establish the complete subject distribution of the entire channel.

### 5.2 Recommended editorial taxonomy

The initial client-proposed categories should be retained as a starting point:

- Money
- Power
- People
- Society
- Life
- Taboos
- Ideas

The final taxonomy should be reviewed against a complete episode inventory.

Each episode should be allowed to belong to multiple topics. The system should avoid forcing a complex conversation into one exclusive category.

For example, a conversation about a business owner's career could be associated with Money, Society, Life, and Ideas where the actual content supports those classifications.

The editorial team should maintain a controlled list of topic names and definitions to avoid creating many near-duplicate categories over time.

### 5.3 Content units

The proposed platform may contain the following content types:

**Episode:** The primary long-form conversation, linked to its original publishing source.

**Guest profile:** A contextual page identifying a guest and linking to their appearances and related content.

**Article or idea:** A written piece developed from an episode, a guest's expertise, a recurring question, or an independently commissioned editorial topic.

**Quote or moment:** A selected passage, short clip, or memorable moment from a conversation. It should include source attribution and a route to the full episode.

**Question of the Week:** A recurring audience-facing prompt designed to encourage reflection or participation.

**Behind-the-scenes material:** Approved photographs, production stories, or contextual material from the work behind an episode.

**Show or series:** A recurring programme or distinct content format, if the catalogue audit confirms that separate shows need their own identity.

**Commercial opportunity:** Information about bookings, sponsorships, advertising, and partnerships.

Not all these content types need to be present at launch. Their inclusion depends on editorial capacity, verified assets, and business priorities.

### 5.4 Repurposing and editorial continuity

The website should support a relationship between long-form episodes and any supporting material developed from them.

A possible content relationship is:

One episode may have several guests, several topics, multiple selected moments, and multiple related articles.

A guest may appear in multiple episodes. A topic may connect many episodes. An article may reference more than one conversation.

These relationships create opportunities for visitors to continue exploring rather than reaching a dead end after consuming one item.

The website should preserve the source episode as the authoritative reference for a repurposed excerpt unless the client establishes a different editorial model.

## 6. Project Objectives

The proposed objectives are grouped by the value they should deliver.

### 6.1 Establish an official digital destination

The website should provide a stable central location for discovering the brand's published work and professional activities.

**Desired outcomes:**

- Visitors can identify the official platform.
- Existing publishing channels are easy to access.
- The website provides context and organisation that individual publishing platforms do not necessarily provide.
- The brand is not dependent on a single third-party discovery interface.

### 6.2 Improve content discovery

Visitors should be able to find relevant material without needing to know an exact episode title.

**Desired outcomes:**

- Search by guest, subject, or keyword.
- Browse by topic.
- Find related conversations.
- Access a useful episode archive.
- Move between a full episode and supporting material.

### 6.3 Extend the value of published conversations

The platform should support the continued usefulness of existing content through articles, selected moments, guest pages, and topic relationships.

**Desired outcomes:**

- Older content remains discoverable.
- Written content provides additional entry points.
- Selected moments lead visitors back to their original conversations.
- Content can be reused in a consistent and attributable way.

### 6.4 Strengthen audience relationships

The website should create legitimate ways for interested visitors to stay connected.

**Desired outcomes:**

- Newsletter subscriptions can be collected with appropriate consent.
- Visitors can submit questions or suggestions where that feature is approved.
- The platform can direct audiences to official viewing and listening destinations.
- Returning visitors can discover new material relevant to their interests.

### 6.5 Support professional and commercial activity

The website should make it easier for relevant organisations and individuals to initiate appropriate enquiries.

**Desired outcomes:**

- Speaking or appearance requests have a clear destination.
- Potential guests can submit proposals.
- Advertisers and partners can enquire about available opportunities.
- Enquiries reach an identified person or team and can be managed consistently.

The existence of these pathways does not establish that every proposed service is currently offered. Commercial offerings, rates, availability, and authorised contacts require confirmation.

## 7. Target Audiences

The initial brief suggests several possible audiences. These should be treated as audience hypotheses until validated through analytics, stakeholder interviews, or audience research.

### 7.1 The curious explorer

**Needs:** Discover interesting questions, people, and subjects without committing to one narrow topic.

**Website expectations:** Clear topic entry points, featured conversations, useful summaries, and recommendations for further exploration.

**Potential success signal:** The visitor opens additional episodes or explores multiple subjects during a visit.

### 7.2 The business-minded audience member

**Needs:** Practical perspectives on money, entrepreneurship, employment, business ownership, and economic opportunity.

**Website expectations:** Searchable business-related conversations, relevant guest profiles, and useful connections between related episodes.

**Potential success signal:** Repeated engagement with relevant episodes and supporting articles.

The platform should not promise financial outcomes or imply that every guest's advice is independently verified.

### 7.3 The socially engaged thinker

**Needs:** Explore politics, culture, public accountability, social behaviour, and competing perspectives.

**Website expectations:** Contextual descriptions, accurate attribution, topic browsing, and access to the full conversation.

**Potential success signal:** Deeper engagement with relevant conversations and related material.

### 7.4 The entertainment-led visitor

**Needs:** Enjoy humour, stories, unexpected moments, and engaging personalities.

**Website expectations:** Easy access to selected moments, compelling episode descriptions, and simple routes to full videos or audio.

**Potential success signal:** Viewing or listening clicks, repeat visits, and exploration beyond the initial item.

### 7.5 The professional or commercial partner

**Needs:** Understand Dr. King’ori's public work and determine whether a booking, sponsorship, advertising placement, or partnership is appropriate.

**Website expectations:** Credible professional information, clearly defined opportunities, and an appropriate enquiry process.

**Potential success signal:** Qualified enquiries that reach the responsible team.

### 7.6 Audience questions requiring validation

Before making firm audience decisions, the client should clarify:

- Which audience is the highest priority?
- Which audiences already account for the largest share of engagement?
- What geographic markets matter most?
- What languages should the website support?
- Is the priority audience primarily mobile?
- What do current viewers and listeners value most?
- Which audience behaviours should the website improve?

The initial brief mentions younger people navigating success and older or established people exploring additional income opportunities. This is a useful starting hypothesis, but age groups, purchasing power, and motivations should not be treated as proven without evidence.

## 9. Functional Requirements

The following requirements establish what the website should do. They do not prescribe how it should look.

Priority definitions:

- **P1 — Launch essential:** Needed to fulfil the core purpose of the first release.
- **P2 — Valuable extension:** Adds meaningful value but can follow the initial launch.
- **P3 — Conditional:** Requires further business, editorial, or technical decisions.

### 9.1 Content archive and discovery

**FR-01 — Episode archive (P1)**  
The website must provide a browsable collection of approved published conversations.

**FR-02 — Episode information (P1)**  
Each episode must support an appropriate title, summary, publication date where known, guest information where applicable, topics, and original source link.

**FR-03 — Topic classification (P1)**  
Episodes must support one or more approved topic classifications.

**FR-04 — Guest relationships (P1)**  
Where guest information is available, visitors should be able to move from a conversation to a guest profile or other appearances.

**FR-05 — Search (P1)**  
Visitors should be able to search the available archive using relevant metadata, subject terms, and episode titles. The final scope depends on catalogue size.

**FR-06 — Filtering (P1/P2)**  
Visitors should be able to filter or browse content by topic. Additional filters, such as date or content format, should be introduced where useful.

**FR-07 — Related content (P2)**  
Episode pages should identify relevant related conversations, articles, or selected moments when such relationships have been reviewed.

**FR-08 — Source linking (P1)**  
Visitors must be able to access the original published media. Embedded media may be used where approved and technically suitable.

### 9.2 Editorial content

**FR-09 — Articles (P2)**  
The platform should support written content with a title, author or attribution where appropriate, publication date, body, topic relationships, and related episodes.

**FR-10 — Quotes and selected moments (P2)**  
Selected material must retain accurate wording or source media, attribution, and a reference to the original conversation where available.

**FR-11 — Guest profiles (P1/P2)**  
Guest profiles should be supported for guests with sufficient approved information. A separate directory interface can be deferred if the initial catalogue is small.

**FR-12 — Content status (P1)**  
The publishing workflow must distinguish draft, review, approved, published, and archived content as appropriate to the selected content management approach.

**FR-13 — Content corrections (P1)**  
Authorised staff must be able to correct metadata and unpublish or archive content when necessary.

### 9.3 Audience engagement

**FR-14 — Newsletter signup (P2)**  
Visitors should be able to subscribe through an approved newsletter provider or equivalent service.

**FR-15 — Consent and unsubscribe (P1 if collecting subscriptions)**  
The newsletter process must record appropriate consent and provide an effective unsubscribe mechanism.

**FR-16 — Question of the Week (P2)**  
The platform may publish a recurring question and invite audience responses if the client approves the format and assigns responsibility for moderation.

**FR-17 — Audience submissions (P3)**  
Any system accepting questions or suggestions should define validation, privacy, moderation, retention, and response expectations before implementation.

### 9.4 Commercial enquiries

**FR-18 — Booking enquiry (P1 if bookings are a launch objective)**  
Visitors must be able to submit a request through a confirmed and monitored channel.

**FR-19 — Guest proposal (P2)**  
If approved, the website should offer a separate way to propose a guest or conversation topic.

**FR-20 — Advertising and partnership enquiry (P1 if commercial lead generation is a launch objective)**  
The website should explain only confirmed opportunities and route enquiries to the responsible contact.

**FR-21 — Enquiry handling (P1 for any active form)**  
Every enquiry form must have an identified recipient, a defined follow-up process, appropriate validation, and a failure-handling procedure.

### 9.5 Navigation and platform integrity

**FR-22 — Consistent navigation (P1)**  
Visitors must be able to reach the archive and primary destinations without relying on a single homepage feature.

**FR-23 — Mobile support (P1)**  
Core browsing, reading, searching, media access, and enquiry tasks must work on supported mobile devices.

**FR-24 — Accessibility (P1)**  
The website must support keyboard navigation, meaningful headings, readable content, appropriate contrast, labelled forms, and accessible media controls or alternatives where applicable.

**FR-25 — Search indexing (P1)**  
Public editorial pages should provide suitable titles, descriptions, canonical URLs where necessary, and indexability controls.

**FR-26 — Analytics (P1)**  
The project must define an appropriate analytics approach to measure discovery, content engagement, media referrals, subscriptions, and enquiries.

**FR-27 — External service resilience (P1)**  
The website should remain useful when an external media platform, embedded player, or third-party service is unavailable. Visitors should have a clear alternative link where possible.

## 10. Key User Journeys

These journeys describe what the website should help visitors accomplish.

### Journey A: Discover a conversation

1. A visitor arrives from a search engine, shared link, or direct visit.
2. The visitor sees a clear description of the conversation or topic.
3. The visitor opens the episode page.
4. The visitor reviews the summary and guest information.
5. The visitor watches or listens through the original platform or an approved embed.
6. The visitor discovers a related conversation or topic.

**Requirement:** Each entry point should provide enough context to help the visitor decide what to explore next.

### Journey B: Explore a subject

1. A visitor is interested in a topic such as money, power, people, or society.
2. The visitor opens the relevant topic page.
3. The visitor browses conversations and supporting articles.
4. The visitor opens a relevant episode.
5. The visitor continues to related material.

**Requirement:** Topics must be meaningfully maintained, not simply attached as unreviewed keywords.

### Journey C: Find a guest

1. A visitor hears about a guest.
2. The visitor searches by name or finds the guest through an episode.
3. The visitor reviews the guest's approved profile and appearances.
4. The visitor opens the relevant conversation.

**Requirement:** Guest names must be accurate and consistently represented across the archive.

### Journey D: Discover a written idea

1. A visitor arrives at an article or selected moment.
2. The visitor understands the subject and source.
3. The visitor reads or watches the material.
4. The visitor follows a reference to the full conversation.
5. The visitor explores related content.

**Requirement:** Repurposed content should preserve attribution and provide meaningful connections to the original material.

### Journey E: Submit a booking enquiry

1. A visitor identifies a relevant professional service.
2. The visitor reviews the available booking information.
3. The visitor submits the required details.
4. The visitor receives an appropriate confirmation.
5. The enquiry reaches the designated person or team.
6. The team follows the agreed response process.

**Requirement:** The website must not promise a response time or availability that the responsible team cannot support.

### Journey F: Propose a guest or topic

1. A visitor identifies a potential guest or discussion subject.
2. The visitor opens the guest proposal route.
3. The visitor submits relevant background and contact details.
4. The proposal is routed to an identified reviewer.
5. The team decides whether and how to respond.

**Requirement:** The website must communicate that submission does not guarantee selection or an invitation.

### Journey G: Explore a commercial opportunity

1. A potential sponsor or partner opens the commercial section.
2. The visitor reviews the confirmed opportunities.
3. The visitor submits an enquiry.
4. The enquiry reaches the responsible contact.
5. The team reviews and follows up.

**Requirement:** The website must distinguish editorial content from paid placement and partnership arrangements.

### Journey H: Subscribe to updates

1. A visitor encounters a newsletter invitation.
2. The visitor understands the type of communication being offered.
3. The visitor submits an email address and required consent.
4. The subscription is processed.
5. The visitor can unsubscribe later.

**Requirement:** The newsletter must have an identified owner, provider, and content plan before it is promoted as an ongoing service.

## 11. Content Management and Editorial Operations

### 11.1 Content ownership

The project needs a clear answer to the question: who is responsible for keeping the website accurate and current?

Possible responsibilities include:

- Maintaining the episode catalogue.
- Verifying guest names and biographies.
- Writing and approving summaries.
- Assigning topics and related content.
- Publishing articles and selected moments.
- Updating booking and commercial information.
- Reviewing enquiries and submissions.
- Correcting errors and removing outdated material.

These responsibilities may belong to one person or several people. They should not be left implicit.

### 11.2 Content workflow

The proposed workflow is:

1. Identify the source material.
2. Confirm its title, guest information, publication details, and source URL.
3. Review the transcript or recording where necessary.
4. Prepare the summary and topic classifications.
5. Verify quotations and factual claims intended for republication.
6. Obtain the required editorial approval.
7. Publish the content.
8. Review links, metadata, and related-content references.
9. Correct or archive the content when necessary.

The client should define which steps are required for different content types. A simple episode metadata update may need a lighter process than a new article involving sensitive claims.

### 11.3 Catalogue audit

Before importing the archive, create a content inventory with, at minimum:

- Source platform.
- Original URL or stable source identifier.
- Episode title.
- Publication date, where known.
- Guest name or names.
- Summary or description.
- Topic classifications.
- Available transcript or supporting text.
- Existing images or thumbnails.
- Rights and permissions status where relevant.
- Proposed website status.
- Verification notes.

The inventory should identify missing metadata, duplicate entries, incorrect titles, uncertain guest names, broken links, and incomplete records.

Automated transcripts should be treated as working references, not automatically approved publication copy.

### 11.4 Existing and future content

The client must decide whether the initial launch will include:

- All historical episodes.
- A selected set of featured episodes.
- Recent episodes only.
- A staged archive that expands over time.

A partial archive is acceptable if it is deliberate and clearly maintained. It should not accidentally imply that it represents the complete catalogue.

The publishing workflow must also define how new episodes are added and who is responsible for doing so.

## 12. Booking, Guest, and Commercial Operations

### 12.1 Professional bookings

The client should confirm which services are actually offered. Potential examples include speaking, event hosting, appearances, moderation, or other professional engagements, but none should be presented as confirmed until approved.

A booking enquiry may need:

- Name.
- Organisation, where applicable.
- Email address.
- Phone number, if required.
- Type of request.
- Event or engagement date.
- Location or delivery format.
- Brief description.
- Relevant budget information, if the client chooses to request it.
- Consent to the applicable data handling terms.

Only collect information necessary to assess and respond to the enquiry.

### 12.2 Guest proposals

Guest proposals serve a different purpose from bookings.

Potential fields include:

- Proposer's name and contact information.
- Proposed guest.
- Guest background.
- Suggested subject or central question.
- Why the conversation may be relevant.
- Supporting links.
- Relationship to the proposed guest, if relevant.

The team must identify who reviews submissions, how often they are reviewed, whether submitters receive a response, and how personal information is handled.

### 12.3 Advertising and partnerships

The commercial section should only describe opportunities that the client has authorised and can fulfil.

Discovery must establish:

- Available advertising formats.
- Sponsorship opportunities.
- Partnership arrangements.
- Audience or performance information that can be shared responsibly.
- Whether a media kit already exists.
- The person responsible for commercial enquiries.
- How requests are assessed and approved.

Any performance figures used in commercial material must have a verifiable source and a clear reporting period.

### 12.4 Sponsorship disclosure

The supplied transcripts contain examples of sponsor or service-provider mentions within conversations. The website should support accurate identification of sponsored material where applicable.

The client must determine the policy for sponsored episodes, paid partnerships, advertising placements, and editorial independence.

The website should not imply that a sponsor endorses every statement in an episode or that a paid relationship has no editorial implications.

## 13. Non-Functional Requirements

These requirements concern the quality, reliability, and maintainability of the website.

### 13.1 Accessibility

The website should aim for WCAG 2.2 Level AA as the accessibility target, subject to final scope and testing.

The implementation should include:

- Semantic page structure.
- Keyboard-operable navigation and controls.
- Visible focus states.
- Form labels and understandable error messages.
- Appropriate text and interface contrast.
- Descriptive link text.
- Alternative text for meaningful images.
- Captions or transcripts where available and appropriate.
- Reduced-motion support if animation is introduced.

Accessibility should be assessed through implementation and testing rather than assumed from the use of a particular framework or component library.

### 13.2 Performance

The website should load efficiently on common mobile devices and variable network conditions.

Performance work should prioritise:

- Efficient image delivery.
- Avoiding unnecessary third-party scripts.
- Lazy loading suitable below-the-fold media.
- Minimising layout shifts.
- Avoiding heavy media embeds that delay access to core content.
- Measuring real performance after deployment.

Performance targets should be agreed before launch and measured against representative pages and devices.

### 13.3 Search engine discoverability

Public content should have meaningful titles, descriptions, stable URLs, and appropriate structured metadata where useful.

The implementation should also consider:

- XML sitemap generation.
- Canonical URLs.
- Social sharing metadata.
- Indexing rules for public and private pages.
- Redirects when URLs change.
- Correct attribution and links to original media.

The site should not create indexable duplicate pages merely because the same episode can be reached through multiple topics.

### 13.4 Security and privacy

Any forms or integrations that collect personal information must have a defined purpose and appropriate protections.

Requirements include:

- Secure handling of submitted information.
- Server-side validation where applicable.
- Protection against spam and abusive submissions.
- Restricted access to private enquiries.
- Appropriate retention and deletion practices.
- Clear privacy information.
- Secure handling of credentials and service integrations.
- A defined process for responding to data incidents.

The actual implementation will depend on the selected platform, hosting environment, form provider, and newsletter service.

### 13.5 Reliability and maintainability

The website should be maintainable by the people responsible for it.

Discovery must establish whether the client expects a content management system, a developer-managed publishing process, or another approved workflow.

The chosen approach must account for:

- Expected publishing frequency.
- Number of people managing content.
- Editorial approval needs.
- Technical support availability.
- Backup and recovery.
- Content export and portability.
- Ongoing service and hosting costs.

No particular CMS or technology stack should be selected solely because it is familiar to the developer.

### 13.6 External platform dependencies

YouTube, Spotify, newsletter providers, analytics tools, and any form or scheduling services introduce external dependencies.

The project should identify which services are essential, who owns the associated accounts, and what happens if an integration is unavailable or a subscription ends.

## 14. Analytics and Success Measurement

Success should be measured against the agreed business objectives rather than raw traffic alone.

### 14.1 Discovery metrics

Potential measures:

- Visits to the episode archive.
- Searches performed.
- Search success or zero-result rate.
- Topic page visits.
- Guest page visits.
- Clicks from topic pages to episode pages.
- Use of related-content links.

### 14.2 Content engagement metrics

Potential measures:

- Episode page engagement.
- Outbound clicks to YouTube and Spotify.
- Plays on embedded media, where measurement is supported.
- Article engagement.
- Visits from selected moments to full episodes.
- Return visits to the website.

Outbound clicks must not be reported as confirmed video or audio completions unless the relevant platform provides reliable evidence.

### 14.3 Audience relationship metrics

Potential measures:

- Newsletter signups.
- Subscription conversion rate.
- Unsubscribe rate.
- Participation submissions, if enabled.

### 14.4 Commercial metrics

Potential measures:

- Booking enquiries received.
- Qualified booking enquiries.
- Guest proposals received.
- Advertising or partnership enquiries.
- Enquiry-to-conversation conversion rate.

### 14.5 Measurement principles

- Which analytics platform will be used.
- Which events will be recorded.
- Who can access reports.
- How privacy and consent requirements apply.
- What baseline data is available.

Targets should be agreed after reviewing available baseline data and realistic operating capacity.

## 16. Risks, Constraints, and Dependencies

### 16.1 Incomplete or inaccurate source metadata

**Risk:** Incorrect episode titles, guest names, publication dates, or summaries reduce trust and damage search discoverability.

**Response:** Audit and verify the catalogue before import. Flag uncertain records rather than silently guessing.

### 16.2 Automated transcription errors

**Risk:** Transcription errors may distort quotations, names, or factual claims.

**Response:** Verify material against the original recording before republishing it as a quotation or factual statement.

### 16.3 Unclear content rights

**Risk:** The website may use photographs, video clips, transcripts, music, logos, or third-party materials without sufficient permission.

**Response:** Establish asset ownership, usage rights, guest permissions where relevant, and attribution requirements before publication.

### 16.4 Unconfirmed brand decisions

**Risk:** Work may proceed using an unapproved public name, programme identity, or relationship between the website and existing shows.

**Response:** Confirm naming and brand hierarchy before final design decisions.

### 16.5 Excessive initial scope

**Risk:** The project becomes difficult to deliver because every proposed feature is treated as a launch requirement.

**Response:** Prioritise the archive and essential user journeys. Defer features that lack an owner, approved workflow, or demonstrated launch necessity.

### 16.6 Unclear editorial responsibility

**Risk:** The archive becomes outdated, content is duplicated, or sensitive material is published without appropriate review.

**Response:** Assign content ownership and establish approval, correction, and removal procedures.

### 16.7 Unmanaged enquiries

**Risk:** Booking or partnership forms collect requests that no one monitors.

**Response:** Confirm the recipient, follow-up process, response expectations, and fallback contact before enabling a form.

### 16.8 Unsupported audience assumptions

**Risk:** The website is structured around assumed age groups or interests that do not reflect the actual audience.

**Response:** Review available channel analytics and obtain stakeholder input before treating audience hypotheses as settled.

### 16.9 Dependence on third-party platforms

**Risk:** Embedded media, external links, or integrations fail or change.

**Response:** Maintain source links, avoid unnecessary dependence on embeds, and test external destinations regularly.

### 16.10 Unclear ongoing budget

**Risk:** Hosting, content management, newsletter services, analytics, and maintenance create unexpected recurring costs.

**Response:** Identify expected operating costs and assign responsibility for ongoing services before launch.

## 19. Final Recommendation

The recommended direction is to develop the website as a **conversation-led editorial platform and central digital home for Dr. King’ori's work**.

The archive should be a foundational capability, not a secondary page added after the homepage has been designed. It gives the platform a durable structure for organising existing conversations and connecting them to guests, subjects, written ideas, and selected moments.

The website should serve both new visitors and established audiences. New visitors need context and clear discovery paths; returning visitors need reliable access to the catalogue and new material. Professional and commercial visitors need credible information and clearly managed enquiry routes.

The initial release should concentrate on the capabilities required to achieve these outcomes. Additional editorial formats, audience participation, and more complex commercial workflows should be introduced when their value, ownership, and operational requirements are established.

The next project stage should begin only after the client reviews this document, confirms the outstanding decisions, and approves the intended scope.

**Discovery principle:** Establish what the platform needs to accomplish, what content and services it can genuinely support, and who is responsible for operating it before deciding how it should look or how it should be built.
